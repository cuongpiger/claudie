## 1. Tax-aware fund placement

**After-tax return is what matters** outside tax-sheltered accounts (like profit
vs. revenue). A fund earning 13%/yr that nets 9% after tax loses to one earning
12% that nets 10%. Don't chase the higher pre-tax number.

- **Your marginal (ordinary income) rate** applies to taxable-bond and money-market
  interest and to **short-term** capital gains (holdings ≤ 1 year). Illustrative
  top rate: **39.6%**.
- **Qualified stock dividends & long-term capital gains** (holdings > 1 year) get
  preferential rates: **0%** in the low brackets (10/15%), **15%** in the middle
  (25–35%), **20%** at the top — plus a **3.8%** surtax on investment income for
  high earners (taxable income > ~$200k single / ~$250k married).
- Bond-fund and money-market "dividends" do **not** qualify for the low dividend
  rate — only dividends from stock-holding funds do.

**Tax-free funds (for taxable money only):**
- **Treasury** funds — dividends federally taxable but **state-tax-free**.
- **Municipal** bond/money funds — **federal-tax-free**; single-state ones are
  **state-tax-free** if you live there.
- These pay lower pre-tax yields, so they only win after tax if your bracket is
  high enough — **consider them at the 28% federal bracket or higher.**

**Tax-friendly stock funds** (no fully tax-free version exists) cut taxable
distributions via buy-and-hold (few capital-gains distributions — index funds and
ETFs excel), long-term gains, and offsetting gains with losses (tax-managed funds).

> [!warning] Two placement rules people get wrong
> - **Never buy tax-free (muni/Treasury) funds inside a retirement account** — the
>   tax shelter is wasted; just chase the highest yield there.
> - **Don't buy a fund right before its distribution** (December is most common).
>   The share price drops by the payout amount, so you gain nothing but still owe
>   tax. Ask the fund company for its estimated year-end distribution when buying
>   late in the year.

General placement heuristic: put tax-inefficient holdings (taxable bonds, REITs,
actively traded funds) inside retirement accounts; hold tax-efficient holdings
(index funds, muni bonds) in taxable accounts.

## 2. Measuring performance: total return

**Total return = dividends + capital gains distributions + share-price change.**
A 15% total return turns $10,000 into $11,500 in a year.

- **Share price alone misleads.** Every distribution *cuts* the share price by
  exactly the payout amount, so a lower price can hide a real gain. Judge by total
  value today vs. total dollars invested — or by benchmark comparison — not by NAV
  alone.
- **Prefer annualized (per-year) returns** over big absolute multi-year numbers for
  comparison (a 98% 5-yr total ≈ 14.6%/yr).
- **Benchmark against the right index**, blending for mixed funds (e.g. an 80/20
  US/foreign stock fund vs. 0.8×US-market + 0.2×EAFE). Indexes carry no expenses,
  so still weigh fees.

## 3. Sell / hold / buy-more decision rules

- **Sell only if** the fund stops meeting the criteria that made it a good buy:
  (1) decent returns vs. its benchmark/peers, (2) fees still competitive, (3) still
  a competent manager/company. One or two soft years are fine if the 5- and 10-year
  numbers hold.
- The other legitimate reason to sell: **your own circumstances/goals changed.**
- Otherwise **hold, and keep feeding good funds.** Don't market-time; don't drift
  into owning ~50 funds.
- **Bear markets:** don't watch too closely; view the *whole* portfolio, not single
  funds; new purchases buy in cheaper; favor value funds + diversify to dampen
  swings. Successful investors check once or twice a year — monthly/quarterly at
  most.

## 4. Rebalancing

Rebalance back toward your target allocation **every 3–5 years** (absent a major
life change), trimming stock exposure gradually as you age. Rebalancing sells what
has run up and buys what's lagged — enforcing "sell high, buy low" mechanically.

## 5. The tax forms (reference)

### Form 1099-DIV (fund distributions; arrives late Jan/early Feb)
| Box | Meaning | Tax treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **1a** Total ordinary dividends | Money-market/bond dividends + **short-term** cap gains | Ordinary rate (up to 39.6%) |
| **1b** Qualified dividends | Corporate stock dividends | 0 / 15 / 20% by bracket |
| **2a** Total capital gains distributions | Long-term gains (> 12 mo) | 0 / 15 / 20% by bracket |
| **3** Nondividend distributions | Return of capital | **Not taxable**, but **reduces your basis** |
| **4** Federal tax withheld | Signals **28% backup withholding** (missing SSN / unreported income) | Credited on your 1040 |
| **6** Foreign tax paid | Tax paid to foreign govts | Recover via **credit** (usually better) or deduction; direct credit if total ≤ $300 ($600 MFJ) |

### Cost basis (when you sell)
**Basis = original investment + all reinvested distributions.** Accounting methods:
- **Specific identification** — you name the exact shares to sell (sell
  highest-cost lots first to minimize current tax). Most control; the book's
  implied preference for tax minimization.
- **FIFO** — IRS default; sells oldest/cheapest first, usually more tax sooner.
- **Average cost** — convenient; required when selling an entire holding.
You can't switch methods once elected for a fund. All gains/losses go on
**Schedule D**.

### Taking gains vs. losses
- Sell largest holdings with smallest gains first; pair gains with losses to offset.
- **Wash-sale rule:** a loss is disallowed if you rebuy the *same* fund within **30
  days** — wait 31+ days or buy a *similar* (not identical) fund.
- **Annual net capital-loss deduction cap: $3,000**; excess carries forward.
- **Form 1099-B** reports sales (with basis for shares acquired 2012+).

### Retirement withdrawals (Form 1099-R)
- Penalty-free withdrawals **after age 59½**; before that, income tax **+ 10%
  federal penalty** (Form 5329) unless an exception (leave work after 55,
  disability, medical > 7.5% AGI, first home up to $10,000, education, death).
- **RMDs must begin by April 1 after age 70½** (traditional IRAs; not Roth) or a
  **50% penalty** applies. Box 1 = gross distribution; Box 2a = taxable amount
  (Form 8606 for nondeductible contributions).

> Don't let tax tactics block a real financial goal — minimize taxes, but the goal
> comes first.
