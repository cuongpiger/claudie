# Is American Funds Growth Fund of America Class A a good deal?

Short version: **Pass on the Class A shares as your broker pitched them.** The fund itself is a respectable, established stock fund from a reputable company — but the 5.75% front-end load is a dealbreaker, and you can get equivalent (or better) diversified stock exposure with no sales commission and a lower expense ratio. Here's the reasoning, run through the three screening filters I use for any specific fund, in order.

## Filter 1 — Costs (the decisive, knowable factor)

You can never know a fund's future return, but you *can* know its costs with certainty, and costs come out of your return every year regardless of how the fund performs. So this is where the decision gets made.

**The load: 5.75% up front — this is the problem.**
A load is a sales commission that goes to the broker recommending it, out of *your* invested dollars. It buys you nothing in fund quality — no study shows load funds get better managers. Watch what it does to your money:

- You hand over **$10,000**. The 5.75% load skims **$575** off the top. Only **$9,425** actually gets invested.
- You are down 5.75% on day one and need the fund to gain about **6.1%** just to claw back to even (since you have to grow the smaller $9,425 base back up to $10,000).
- That's a permanent, guaranteed drag before the market does anything.

A common counter you may hear from the broker: *"the fund company pays my commission, not you."* Not true — it comes straight out of the amount you invest. There is simply no reason to pay this when equivalent no-load funds exist.

**The expense ratio: 0.64% per year — the less bad part.**
For an actively managed US stock fund this is actually below the old active-fund average (~1.25%), and American Funds is known for keeping expenses relatively moderate. So 0.64% on its own wouldn't be a red flag. But context matters: a plain broad-market US stock **index fund runs around ~0.03%–0.15%**. On a stock fund, an extra ~0.5%/yr of expense is roughly 5% of your expected annual return handed over every year, compounding for decades. Combined with the 5.75% load, the total cost gap versus a no-load index alternative is large.

**Cost verdict: fails filter 1.** Not because 0.64% is outrageous, but because the 5.75% load is pure avoidable waste and there's no need to accept it.

## Filter 2 — Performance vs. risk

Growth Fund of America is one of the largest, longest-running US growth funds, with a genuinely long track record — so unlike a hyped "hot fund," it's not a one-year flash you should be suspicious of. That's a point in its favor.

But two cautions:
- **Long-run, most active funds trail a comparable low-cost index** after costs, largely *because* of costs — and here you'd be starting each dollar 5.75% behind. The load makes it even harder for the fund to beat a cheap index net of what you paid.
- Judge any return against the *right* benchmark (a broad US large-cap/growth index), not a flattering one. Even in years the fund looks good gross, the load and higher expense ratio eat the edge.

So the fund isn't reckless or gimmicky — the issue is that its cost structure makes market-beating net returns unlikely, not that it takes wild risk.

## Filter 3 — Manager & company

This is where the fund scores well. American Funds (Capital Group) is an experienced, deep, reputable fund company with real category expertise and a team-based management approach, not a marketing-department gimmick. If the *only* question were "is this a legitimate, well-run fund," the answer would be yes.

That's not enough to save it, though: the gap between a good manager and an average one is small (a couple percent a year at most), while the 5.75% load is a large, certain cost. Good management doesn't justify an unnecessary sales commission.

## Verdict: Pass — and here's the single reason

**The 5.75% front-end load is an avoidable cost that buys you nothing.** You lose $575 of every $10,000 before you're even invested, and you can get equivalent diversified US stock exposure with **no load** and a **much lower expense ratio**.

What to look at instead:
- A broad **no-load US stock index fund** (total-market or S&P 500) — expense ratios around 0.03%–0.15%, no commission. This is the low-cost core most long-term stock money should sit in.
- If you specifically want *active* management from American Funds, know that the **same fund and manager** are often available without the Class A sales charge inside many **401(k)/employer plans** or through certain no-load/institutional share classes — so you may not need to pay the load even for this exact fund. Ask specifically about a no-load or load-waived share class.
- Be skeptical of the Class **B and C** shares if the broker offers those as an "alternative" — they dodge the upfront load but pile on higher annual 12b-1 fees that usually cost you more over time.

One thing to confirm before you act: get the fund's **prospectus** and read the line "Total Fund Operating Expenses" and the sales-charge schedule yourself — marketing materials obscure true costs, and the prospectus is the only reliable source.
