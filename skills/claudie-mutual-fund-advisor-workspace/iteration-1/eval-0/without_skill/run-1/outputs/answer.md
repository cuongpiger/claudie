# How to Split Your $85k IRA

First, congratulations on the rollover — consolidating an old 401(k) into an IRA is a smart move that usually gives you lower fees and far more fund choices.

A quick disclaimer: I'm not a licensed financial advisor, and this isn't personalized investment advice. What follows is a widely used framework for someone in your situation. Consider running it past a fee-only (fiduciary) advisor before acting.

## Your profile in a nutshell

- **Age 42, ~25-year horizon.** That's a long runway. Time is your biggest asset — it lets you ride out market downturns and lets compounding do the heavy lifting.
- **"Middle-of-the-road" risk.** For a moderate investor with 25 years to go, a common starting point is roughly **75–80% stocks / 20–25% bonds**. You're still young enough to lean growth-heavy, but the bond slice keeps volatility from feeling stomach-churning.

A rough rule of thumb some people use is "110 minus your age" for the stock percentage → 110 − 42 = **68% stocks**. A more growth-oriented "120 minus age" gives **78%**. Given your long horizon and the fact that you won't touch this money for decades, landing somewhere in the **75/25** range is reasonable for a moderate risk tolerance.

## A simple, low-cost model portfolio (~75/25)

The single most important thing you can control is **cost**. Favor low-expense-ratio index funds (look for expense ratios under ~0.10%). Here's a classic "three-to-four fund" split:

| Allocation | Fund type | What it covers |
|-----------:|-----------|----------------|
| **45%** | Total U.S. Stock Market index | Broad exposure to the entire U.S. market (large, mid, small cap) |
| **25%** | Total International Stock index | Developed + emerging markets outside the U.S. |
| **5%**  | (Optional) U.S. Small-Cap or REIT index | A modest tilt for extra diversification/growth |
| **25%** | Total Bond Market index | Stability and ballast |

Rounded, that's **~75% stocks / 25% bonds**, globally diversified.

### If you want maximum simplicity

You could put the entire $85k into a **single Target-Date Retirement fund** — for you, that'd be a **Target 2050** fund (you're ~65 around 2049–2050). It automatically holds a diversified stock/bond mix and gradually gets more conservative as you approach retirement. One fund, one decision, professionally rebalanced. Just check that the version you pick is a low-cost index-based one, since target-date funds vary a lot in fees.

### If you want to build it yourself

Any of the big low-cost providers (Vanguard, Fidelity, Schwab) offer near-identical total-market index funds. Example building blocks (not endorsements — just illustrative of the categories):

- Total U.S. stock: e.g. VTI / FSKAX / SWTSX
- Total international: e.g. VXUS / FTIHX / SWISX
- Total bond: e.g. BND / FXNAX / SWAGX

## In dollar terms ($85,000 at 75/25)

- U.S. stocks (45%): ~$38,250
- International stocks (25%): ~$21,250
- Small-cap/REIT tilt (5%, optional): ~$4,250
- Bonds (25%): ~$21,250

(If you skip the small-cap tilt, just fold that 5% into the U.S. stock fund → 50% U.S. stocks.)

## A few practical tips

1. **Rebalance about once a year.** If stocks run up and your mix drifts to, say, 82/18, sell a bit and buy bonds to get back to target. Inside an IRA this is tax-free, so it's painless.
2. **Automate contributions if you can.** You can generally add up to the annual IRA limit ($7,000 in 2025 if under 50; check the current year's cap). Dollar-cost averaging over time smooths out market timing risk.
3. **Don't tinker during downturns.** Your 25-year horizon means you'll live through several bear markets. The investors who do best are usually the ones who leave it alone.
4. **Reassess your bond percentage every 5–10 years.** As you get within ~10 years of retirement, gradually shift more toward bonds (a target-date fund does this for you automatically).
5. **Mind the fees.** Over 25 years, the difference between a 0.05% and a 1.0% expense ratio can cost you tens of thousands of dollars. Cheap index funds win.

## Bottom line

For a 42-year-old moderate investor with a 25-year horizon, a globally diversified **~75% stock / 25% bond** portfolio built from low-cost index funds is a sound, mainstream approach. If you'd rather not manage it, a single low-cost **Target-Date 2050** fund gets you essentially the same thing on autopilot.

Whatever you choose, the winning behaviors are the same: keep costs low, stay diversified, rebalance occasionally, and don't panic-sell. Time in the market is on your side.
